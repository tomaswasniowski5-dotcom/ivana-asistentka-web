What makes it different

Depth doesn’t mean force

In eight years of practice I’ve seen one thing again and again: chronically overloaded structures release more deeply and more quickly when the client is as relaxed as possible during the treatment – even while I’m working on a painful spot.

The effectiveness of manual work doesn’t depend on pressure alone. What matters most is the state your nervous system is in during the treatment.

When the body starts to guard – a muscle tenses, the breath stops – I don’t push harder against your resistance. Instead, I bring in breathwork and shifts in intensity. Paradoxically, this raises your tolerance threshold, and I can return to the problem area with far greater effect.

Not every muscle tension is a problem. The body is an adaptive system.

Telling functional tone apart from genuine overload is part of everything I do.

For the curious: the method in detail

Method name: Deep Recovery Treatment – in-depth manual bodywork.

Techniques

Deep treatment of overloaded muscles: deep tissue, trigger point therapy, myofascial release and spinal mobilisation techniques. A somatic approach to the nervous system: pain window titration, pendulation and breathwork.

What the method is based on

When treating chronically overloaded structures, deeper and faster release has repeatedly been achieved when the client was in the highest possible state of subjective relaxation. The effectiveness of manual work depends directly on the state of the client’s nervous system during treatment, not merely on the intensity of the pressure applied.

If muscle guarding, breath-holding or an overall defensive response of the body appears, pendulation and breathwork take over instead of increasing intensity against the client’s resistance. These shift the tolerance threshold and, through targeted titration of pressure, make it possible to return to the affected area with greater effectiveness.

Each treatment is always based on the client’s individual needs and context – their training load, movement patterns and the current state of their nervous system.
